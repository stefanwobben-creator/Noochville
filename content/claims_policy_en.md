Anyone may publish a sustainability claim, provided that all four of these hold at the moment it goes live.

## Four conditions

- **On the list.** The claim, in the wording used, has a green verdict in the Nooch claims register. Not in the register means not live until Compliance has judged it.
- **Evidence with the claim.** The substantiation is findable at the place of the claim itself, not only in the FAQ.
- **Scoped.** The claim names the material, component or stage it covers. "100%" only after a material-by-material check.
- **Alive.** When the certificate, standard or lab result behind a claim expires or changes, the claim comes down or is reworded.

## Never, whatever the evidence (EU-blacklist, Annex I UCPD)

- A generic environmental claim without demonstrated recognised excellent environmental performance.
- Climate neutrality or improved climate impact that rests on offsetting instead of the product's own life cycle.
- A sustainability label not backed by a certification scheme or a public authority. A badge we made ourselves is not a label.
- A claim about the whole product, brand or company that is true of only one aspect of it.

## Who decides

- **Green:** any role, wording unchanged.
- **Orange:** Compliance approves the wording first and records the evidence with it.
- **Red:** not used. A reformulation is a new claim and starts again at condition one.
- **No hard legal basis:** the checker does not decide. Compliance does, and writes down the reason.

This policy governs whether a claim may be published, not whether it is true. Voice and tone stay with the copy rules.
