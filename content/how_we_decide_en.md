This page is mostly what sits below it: the decisions people here actually made, with the assumptions they rested on and the predictions they wrote down. The nine lines above that are the same method the decision coach walks you through, so if you would rather be asked than read, open the coach and skip the list.

- **0. Check the question first.** Half of a bad decision is a good answer to the wrong question: "which outsole supplier" is often really "do we change the outsole at all this season".
- **1. Name the option you left out.** Doing nothing and waiting are options too, and they are the ones that never put themselves on the list.
- **2. Say what would change your mind.** If no fact could move you, you are defending a choice, not making one.
- **3. Look at how these usually go.** Before arguing why our case is different, ask what normally happens when a small brand adds a second factory.
- **4. Weigh what waiting costs.** A decision you can undo in a week deserves a morning. One that locks in a mould for a year deserves a week.
- **5. Imagine it failed, and say why.** It is twelve months later and the launch flopped. Write the reason down now, while you can still act on it.
- **6. Ask who benefits, you included.** The option that is most interesting to work on is not always the option that is best for Nooch.
- **7. Write the prediction down, with a number and a date.** "This will do well" cannot be wrong. "40 pairs by 1 March" can, and that is the point.
- **8. Decide now what makes you stop.** Pick the signal in advance, because afterwards you will find reasons to keep going.
